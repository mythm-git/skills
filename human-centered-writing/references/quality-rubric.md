# Quality Rubric

Use this rubric only when the user requests an evaluation. It is an editorial aid, not a scientific detector or authorship measurement. Score each applicable dimension from 0–10 and cite observable evidence from the text. Aim for 8+ where the brief makes the dimension applicable.

| Dimension | Observable criteria |
|---|---|
| Naturalness | wording and flow fit a plausible writer and situation; no needless generic framing or forced variation |
| Voice consistency | register, perspective, vocabulary, and pacing fit the supplied sample or stated preference |
| Contextual appropriateness | tone, structure, and detail fit audience, genre, and language conventions |
| Sentence rhythm | sentence and paragraph shape serve meaning; no monotonous or mechanically varied pacing |
| Vocabulary appropriateness | terms are accurate, concrete when evidence allows, and suitable for reader and discipline |
| Formatting restraint | headings, lists, emphasis, and punctuation earn their place and preserve required markup |
| Clarity and conciseness | main point, logic, and next action are easy to find without unnecessary repetition |
| Semantic fidelity | revised meaning, facts, quotations, citations, figures, dates, and constraints remain intact |

**Hard requirements:** semantic fidelity, protected-detail preservation, and adherence to explicit user constraints. A high stylistic score cannot compensate for failure in any hard requirement.

For a paired model comparison, evaluate baseline and skill-enabled outputs against the same brief. Record the model, settings, exact prompts, raw outputs, and each protected-detail check before assigning scores. Compare naturalness, AI-associated patterns, punctuation and formatting, sentence structure, vocabulary, voice preservation, and semantic accuracy. A failed hard requirement is a failure even if the prose is otherwise polished. Do not infer authorship or predict detector outcomes.
