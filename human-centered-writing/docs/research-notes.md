# Research Notes: Detection, Provenance, and Responsible Writing

## Design conclusion

This skill focuses on writing quality rather than detector outcomes. AI-authorship classifiers and provenance mechanisms have material limitations, and neither an “AI” score nor a “human” score proves authorship. The responsible response is to preserve evidence of one’s process when provenance matters: drafts, source notes, citations, and revision history.

## What published sources support

- OpenAI retired its public text classifier in 2023 because of its low accuracy. Its published limitations state that classifiers can mislabel human text, are weak on short text, and should not be primary decision-making tools.
- Turnitin describes its AI-writing report as an indicator for educator review and suppresses low-percentage results to reduce the risk of false positives.
- Research on detection and watermarking shows that detection results depend on method, text length, transformation, and system design. These constraints do not justify trying to manipulate scores.

## Skill implications

The core instructions therefore:

1. preserve facts and cite sources;
2. make prose appropriate for the reader rather than statistically unusual;
3. prohibit manipulating provenance, watermarks, or detector scores;
4. recommend process documentation where authorship matters;
5. avoid claims that a style can prove or disprove AI involvement.
